August 1, 2026 — United Airlines has begun replacing its signature transparent blue plastic cups with a new recyclable paper “universal cup” as part of its efforts to reduce single-use plastics across its onboard operations.

The rollout started on August 1, 2026, on international flights and United Express services. The new cups will expand to all United mainline domestic flights beginning October 1, 2026.
The new paper cup is designed to serve both hot and cold beverages, replacing United’s previous system, which used separate paper cups for hot drinks and plastic cups for cold beverages.

United expects the initiative to eliminate approximately 250 million single-use plastic cups every year, equivalent to around 1,260 metric tons of plastic.

According to the airline, the universal cup consists of two layers of paper with a water-based interior coating. It is certified as both recyclable and compostable and is designed to work with United’s existing mixed-material recycling streams on domestic routes.

United said the amount of plastic eliminated is roughly equivalent to the weight of 13.5 Airbus A321neo aircraft.

The new cup also complies with single-use plastic regulations in several markets, including Canada, the European Union, Hong Kong and India. This allows United to standardize its beverage service across multiple jurisdictions rather than using different cup formats to meet local requirements.

The development of the universal cup began as a cross-functional initiative to identify a single beverage container capable of safely serving hot drinks, cold beverages and alcoholic drinks without compromising the passenger experience or operational requirements.

For an airline operating thousands of flights across a global network, the change also has an important operational dimension. Using one cup for multiple beverage categories means United can eliminate the need to stock separate containers for hot and cold drinks.

This standardization is expected to simplify onboard catering and inventory management, reduce the number of service items carried on aircraft and streamline galley operations.

United is also introducing updated onboard recycling bags designed to make it easier for passengers and cabin crew to identify recyclable items.

The initiative follows another development in United’s recycling program: the airline became the first carrier to receive approval from U.S. Customs and Border Protection to recycle waste from international flights arriving at Los Angeles International Airport (LAX).

The new universal cup also has a larger capacity than the plastic cup it replaces, giving United an additional opportunity to standardize beverage service while reducing material use.

For United, the project illustrates how relatively small changes to onboard service equipment can have a significant impact when applied across a large airline network. A single change to a high-volume catering item can eliminate hundreds of millions of disposable products every year.

The universal cup is part of United’s broader sustainability strategy, which focuses on reducing single-use plastics while expanding recycling capabilities across its onboard operations.
