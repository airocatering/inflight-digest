On March 16, 2026, Russian President Vladimir Putin authorized Aeroflot to acquire the remaining 49% stake in Aeromar held by Truffle 2 GmbH, a Lufthansa-linked company, paving the way for Aeroflot to become the sole owner of Russia’s largest inflight catering company.

The presidential authorization clears the final regulatory hurdle for a transaction that has been in the making for several years. Aeroflot already owns 51% of Aeromar, while the remaining 49% has been held by Truffle 2 GmbH, a company associated with Lufthansa.

The transaction covers 4,900 ordinary shares of Aeromar. Once completed, Aeroflot will consolidate full ownership of the company, bringing one of Russia’s most important airline catering operations entirely under domestic ownership.

Aeromar is a major player in Russia’s inflight catering market, providing meals and related services to airlines operating through Moscow and other Russian airports. Its customer base has historically included Aeroflot Group carriers as well as other airlines.

The sale of Lufthansa’s stake has its roots in the restructuring of the German group’s activities in Russia. Lufthansa had sought to exit its investment in Aeromar for several years, with the transaction becoming increasingly complicated by sanctions, ownership restrictions and the regulatory environment surrounding foreign assets in Russia.

For Aeroflot, full ownership of Aeromar provides greater control over a strategically important part of its supply chain. Airline catering is closely integrated with flight operations, requiring specialized production facilities, airport access, logistics infrastructure and strict just-in-time delivery.

The deal also marks another step in the withdrawal of Lufthansa-linked interests from the Russian aviation market. Following the transaction, Lufthansa will no longer have an equity position in Aeromar.

The acquisition illustrates how ownership of critical aviation infrastructure has been reshaped since the beginning of Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine. Companies operating in areas such as catering, ground handling, maintenance and airport services have increasingly become subject to government scrutiny and restrictions on foreign ownership.

For Aeroflot, meanwhile, the acquisition strengthens vertical integration around its core airline operations. Controlling Aeromar outright gives the group direct control over catering capacity, production planning and a key element of the passenger experience.

The transaction is therefore more than a simple change in shareholding: it represents the final stage of Lufthansa’s exit from Aeromar and the transformation of the company into a fully Aeroflot-controlled inflight catering operation.
